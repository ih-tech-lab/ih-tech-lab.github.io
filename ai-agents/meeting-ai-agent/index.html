Meeting AI Agent: questions from business leaders

How quickly will we see results?

Your team tests the agent on real meetings within the first weeks. Most organisations see the time saved on scheduling and preparation, and more actions closed on time, during the first month after going live.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many people and meetings it supports, the systems it connects to and where it runs. After a first conversation you get a written proposal with the price, so you can compare it with what the work costs you today.

Will it replace our coordinators and secretaries?

It takes over the chasing, copying and reminding, so the same people can support more meetings and spend their time on the content. What goes on the agenda, what is shared and what is decided stays with your people.

What if a brief contains a wrong figure?

It takes figures only from your own reports and systems, shows the source and date of each one, and nothing is shared until the person who owns the meeting approves it. Every brief and action is logged, so you can review and correct it.

Can it reschedule meetings on its own?

Only if you allow it. Otherwise it suggests new times and the organiser confirms. You decide what it may book, send and post alone, and you can pause it at any time.

Is our information safe?

It can run on your own servers, with its AI models, so board papers, notes and figures stay inside your organisation and no outside AI service sees them. We have no access to it, and your data is never used to train AI models.

Do we have to change our calendars or tools?

No. It works with Outlook and Google Calendar, your room booking system, Microsoft Teams, Slack, Jira, Asana, Notion and Confluence.

Does it sit in on our meetings?

No. It never joins a meeting. It works in writing only: calendars, briefs, documents, the notes your team shares afterwards and the tasks that follow.

What do you need from our team?

One person who knows how your meetings are organised today, access to test versions of the calendars and systems involved, and a few hours a week from the team while we test.

Can we start small?

Yes. Most organisations start with one recurring meeting, such as a management or committee meeting, see the results, and then extend the agent to other teams.
